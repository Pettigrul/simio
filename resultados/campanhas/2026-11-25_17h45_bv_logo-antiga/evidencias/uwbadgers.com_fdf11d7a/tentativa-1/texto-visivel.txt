Quick six: No. 2 Badgers host Penn State for final home B1G series of 2026

November 19, 2026 | Men's Hockey

Share:

Fans encouraged to 'Red Out' the Kohl Center on Saturday

By: Hannah Sarnoff

Buy Tickets: Friday | Saturday

MADISON, Wis. – The No. 2 Wisconsin men's hockey team continues play at the Kohl Center and plays host to Penn State on Friday at 7 p.m. and Saturday at 6 p.m. The series marks the final Big Ten home series until January. 

The second-ranked Badgers are 8-1-1 overall and sit in first place with 14 points and a 4-1-1 record in the Big Ten.

The Badgers will wear red uniforms in both games this weekend, with Saturday a 'RED OUT' with fans encouraged to wear red to the game.

Game Notes
Rosters: Wisconsin | Penn State

Here are six things to know before game day:

1. Tables have turned – Junior transfer JJ Wiebusch faces his former team for the first time since committing to Wisconsin in April. In eight career games against the Badgers with Penn State, he had one goal and three points. 

This season Wiebusch has tallied 11 points, three goals, eight assists. His last goal came last weekend against Ohio State in the Badgers' first loss of the season.

Wiebusch has points in five consecutive games with 10 points during that stretch (3g, 7a). That serves as the longest active streak among Badgers.

2. Winning Wooten – Three of freshman forward Zach Wooten's four goals have served as game-winning goals this season, including last Friday's against Ohio State in the 5-2 victory.

Wooten's three winning goals share third in the nation and are already tied for second among Badgers wearing a Wisconsin uniform behind junior forward Ryan Botterill's four game-winning markers. Gavin Morrissey also owns three game-winning goals. 

Junior transfer JJ Wiebusch boasts five career winning tallies, but all five were for Penn State.

3. National ranks – Wisconsin leads the country with a 3.20 scoring margin and ranks sixth nationally with 4.20 goals per game. Sophomore forward Bruno Idžan leads the team with five goals. Freshman forwards Brent Solomon and Zach Wooten and sophomore defenseman Luke Osburn are tied for second with four apiece.

UW is outscoring opponents 17-5 on special teams with 15 power-play goals and two short-handed goals scored, and five power-play goals against. UW's +12 special teams mark is the best in the country, two better than Denver and Minnesota Duluth.

The Badgers started the season unbeaten in nine games (8-0-1) for the third time in school history (2025-26, 1981-82). UW dropped the 10th game, but started the season with the second-best streak behind only the 1981-82 team's 14-game run (13-0-1). Both the 1981-82 and 2025-26 teams reached the NCAA championship game.

4. Looking back – The road team has won the last 10 games in the series between Wisconsin and Penn State. The Badgers look to put an end to their four-game Kohl Center losing streak to PSU this weekend, though they do own six-straight wins in the series against the Nittany Lions on the road. The last time the Nittany Lions visited the Kohl Center last January, the Badgers fell 7-2 in the opener and 3-1 in the second game. UW was missing significant personal for the series including forwards Gavin Morrissey, Vasily Zelenov and Finn Brink, as well as defenseman Zach Schulz.

5. Scouting the Nittany Lions – Penn State is 2-9-1 overall and 0-5-1 in the Big Ten. Maine transfer and sophomore forward Justin Poirer leads Penn State with seven goals and 15 points through 12 games, while sophomore defenseman Jackson Smith has three goals and 14 points to rank second on the team in scoring.
 
In net, sophomore Josh Fleming and freshman Ryan Fetterolf have split the duties.

6. How to watch – The best way to follow along is in person at the Kohl Center. Both games stream on B1G+ and air on the Badger Radio Network. Follow along on X and Instagram at @BadgerMHockey.
