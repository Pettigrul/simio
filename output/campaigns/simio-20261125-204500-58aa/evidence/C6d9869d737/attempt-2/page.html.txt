                                                <a href="https://katten.com/insights/advisories/commerce-department-releases-new-export-rules-for-advanced-ai-chips-and-data-centers" tabindex="-1">Commerce Department Releases New Export Rules for Advanced AI Chips and Data Centers</a>
                                                
                                            </h3>
                                            
                                            <div class="slide__content"><p>On November 16, the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) released “Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors,” an interim final rule (IFR) to address remote access to advanced artificial intelligence (AI) chips and servers. According to BIS, the IFR was written by and at the direction of the Department of the Treasury and the National Security Council, and is primarily aimed at stopping companies in China, among other restricted countries, from renting large amounts of US-based AI compute from data centers in third countries.&nbsp;<br>
The good news for data centers in allied countries is that the rule does not ban US chips; it does, however, require a license whenever a supplier knows that a data center will provide more than 12,000,000 aggregate total processing performance (TPP) of compute to or for a company headquartered in China, Macau or another Country Group D:5 destination (see “Restricted Countries” below). The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, including its country tiers and Data Center Validated End-User program.</p>

                                                <a href="https://katten.com/insights/advisories/commerce-department-releases-new-export-rules-for-advanced-ai-chips-and-data-centers" tabindex="-1">Commerce Department Releases New Export Rules for Advanced AI Chips and Data Centers</a>
                                                
                                            </h3>
                                            
                                            <div class="slide__content"><p>On November 16, the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) released “Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors,” an interim final rule (IFR) to address remote access to advanced artificial intelligence (AI) chips and servers. According to BIS, the IFR was written by and at the direction of the Department of the Treasury and the National Security Council, and is primarily aimed at stopping companies in China, among other restricted countries, from renting large amounts of US-based AI compute from data centers in third countries.&nbsp;<br>
The good news for data centers in allied countries is that the rule does not ban US chips; it does, however, require a license whenever a supplier knows that a data center will provide more than 12,000,000 aggregate total processing performance (TPP) of compute to or for a company headquartered in China, Macau or another Country Group D:5 destination (see “Restricted Countries” below). The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, including its country tiers and Data Center Validated End-User program.</p>
